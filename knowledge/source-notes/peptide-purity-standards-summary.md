Source 1:
Title: Thermo Fisher custom peptide synthesis brochure
URL: https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/brochures/custom-peptide-synthesis-brochure.pdf
Platform: Thermo Fisher Scientific
Tier: clinical_reference
Topic: peptide purity standards

Plain-English source notes:
Thermo Fisher's custom peptide synthesis brochure lists standard custom peptide synthesis capabilities including purity options from crude to greater than 98% pure, with MS quality control for crude peptides and MS plus HPLC for all other purity grades. Education point: reputable synthesis providers treat purity as an orderable grade, not one universal number. HPLC plus mass spectrometry identity confirmation matters more than a purity percentage alone.

Source 2:
Title: BioCat peptide purity guideline
URL: https://www.biocat.com/peptide-synthesis/peptide-purity-guideline
Platform: BioCat
Tier: clinical_reference
Topic: peptide purity standards

Plain-English source notes:
BioCat's peptide synthesis guideline lists recommended peptide purity levels including crude or higher by HPLC, greater than 95% by HPLC, and greater than 98% by HPLC. Education point: 95% is a common lower research-grade specification, while greater than 98% is a higher-purity grade for more demanding uses.

Plain-English source notes:
Several current MOTS-c research-peptide listings and COAs advertise or specify 98% to 99%+ purity by HPLC, often paired with mass spectrometry identity confirmation and sometimes endotoxin testing. Examples found during May 2026 review included MOTS-c listings or COAs at 99%+, 99.64%, 99.84%, 99.9%, and 99.94%. Education point: for a premium customer-facing research peptide standard, Theo should prefer "target >=99% HPLC purity with identity confirmation by mass spectrometry and batch-specific COA" while acknowledging that 95% can appear as a lower research-grade spec.
